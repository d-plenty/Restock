# Privacy policy

_Last updated 9 November 2026_

**Privacy policy? There's nothing to collect, so there's nothing to hide from you. But here's the long version anyway.**

Restock is a free, open-source app that helps you spend less on groceries. It isn't here to take advantage of you. There's no account, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking, and the people who make Restock never receive your data. All the code is public, so anyone can check that this is true.

## In short

- Everything you put in Restock stays on your phone.
- No account, no ads, no analytics, no tracking.
- Permissions are only used for the feature that asks for them, and only when you use it.
- The app talks directly to the stores and a few open services, never to us.
- If you turn on sync, your household's phones share your Restock data straight with each other over your Wi-Fi, encrypted, or through a folder you pick, sealed so only your phones can read it. It never goes through us.
- Sharing prices about products is on by default, but for now they're only saved in your own backups. Nothing is sent anywhere, and nothing about you is in them.

## What stays on your phone

Your pantry, keg and pharmacy items, shopping lists, the shops you've picked or added yourself, price history, loyalty card numbers, household name, categories and settings are stored in a database on your phone. They never leave it unless you export a backup, or turn on sync, which copies them to the other phones in your household (see Sync between your phones).

Uninstalling Restock, or clearing its storage in Android settings, deletes all of it.

"Erase everything and start fresh" in Settings deletes all of it from this phone too. It also signs you out of any shop you signed in to inside Restock, and clears the files Restock exported (its own copies of the backups, spreadsheets and diagnostics files it handed to Android's share menu). Files you already saved or sent somewhere else stay where you put them.

Price history is kept for as long as you choose in Settings: 8 weeks unless you change it, anywhere from 1 month to 6 months. Older prices are deleted from the phone automatically.

Receipts you keep (the photo, shop, date, total and the words read off it) are stored inside Restock's own storage, not in your phone's photo gallery. They're deleted automatically after the time you choose, counted from the day you scanned each one: 3 months unless you change it, or kept until you delete them. Pinned receipts are kept.

If you turn on the Keg tab, Restock asks you to confirm you're 18 or over. That answer is kept only on your phone, like everything else.

## What we collect

Nothing. Restock has no server run by its makers, no sign-in, no crash reporting, no analytics and no advertising. We can't see what you buy, where you shop or what you search for. Shared prices (below) are, for now, only saved in your own backups.

## Permissions and what they're for

Internet: to look up prices and stock, and for the services listed below.

Camera: to scan barcodes and QR codes, and to photograph a receipt or the front of a pack if you use those. It's only on while you're using it. Barcode and pack pictures are read on your phone and not kept; receipt photos are kept only inside Restock, for as long as you choose. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Approximate location: only when you tap "Use my location" to find nearby stores, bottle shops or pharmacies. Restock asks Android once, uses the result to look up stores, and keeps only the area name and position on your phone. It never tracks you in the background, and never asks for precise location.

Notifications, and alarms & reminders: to tell you about deals and send the reminders you choose. They're made on your phone.

Vibration: a small buzz when you add something to your list.

Wi-Fi network discovery, staying ready in the background, and starting after a restart: only if you turn on sync. Restock uses them to find your household's other phones on your Wi-Fi and to sync with them while it's closed. While it's ready to sync, Android shows a quiet notification. Restock may also ask to be left out of Android's battery saving, so sync keeps working while it's closed.

Running in the background and keeping the phone awake: only while a search test you started in Diagnostics is running, so it can carry on with the screen off. A notification shows how far it's got, with a Stop button.

Every permission is optional. Turn any of them off in Android settings and the rest of the app keeps working.

Photos: there's no photos permission. If you import a barcode from a picture, Android's photo picker hands Restock only the one picture you choose. It's read on your phone to find the code and isn't kept.

## Services the app talks to, straight from your phone

The shops you pick (Coles, Woolworths and Aldi, and any bottle shops, pharmacies or shop websites you add): when you search, add an item, check prices or open a product's details, your phone asks that shop's website for product names, prices and stock, just like visiting the site. Searches only go to the shops on the tab you're using, so a grocery search never goes to a bottle shop or pharmacy. The shop sees an ordinary visit from your phone's internet connection, and may set its usual cookies. Sometimes Restock opens the shop's website in a hidden built-in browser to do this; the shop's own website code, including its analytics, runs there as it would in any browser. Each shop's own privacy policy applies to these visits.

Your Woolworths account, only if you sign in to it inside Restock: you sign in on Woolworths' own page in Restock's built-in browser, so your email and password go to Woolworths, never to Restock. Restock then asks Woolworths' website for your store's prices and stock, and can add items to your trolley, the way the website does. Restock keeps only whether you're signed in and which store your account uses. Stop using it any time in Settings → My shops, and sign out on the Woolworths page to end the session there. Your Coles store number, if you give it, is sent to Coles with each search so the prices and stock are for that store.

Diagnostics recordings, only when you start one: while you add something to a shop's trolley yourself, Restock notes what that shop's own page asks its servers. Sign-in, account, payment and tracking calls are left out, and names, emails, phone numbers, addresses, card and member numbers, and the values in web addresses (such as search words) are blanked. The recording stays on your phone unless you choose to share it.

Search test, "Send search details" and "Wrong results?" files, only when you make one: the words searched and what the shops sent back for them, saved in a file on your phone. Nothing from your pantry, lists or settings is in it, and "Send search details" searches the shop's online store, not your branch. A shop's answer can still be about you: if you're signed in to Woolworths inside Restock, Woolworths answers for your account (its store's prices and stock, and possibly what's in your trolley), so check the file before you share it. It goes nowhere unless you choose to share it.

Open Food Facts, Open Beauty Facts and Open Products Facts (openfoodfacts.org, openbeautyfacts.org and openproductsfacts.org), the Open Food Facts project's free, open product databases: when you scan a barcode or open a product's details, the barcode number is sent to look up the product's name, ingredients, allergens and nutrition. Restock asks the one most likely to know it first (Open Beauty Facts on the Pharmacy tab, for example), and the others only if it doesn't. Nothing else is sent.

Barcodes at the supermarkets: if those databases don't know a barcode well enough, Restock may search Woolworths and Coles for it to find the product's name, but only the ones you've switched on, and without your sign-in. It first asks the shop directly from your phone, with a separate set of cookies kept only in your phone's memory and deleted when Restock closes or when you use Erase everything, never the cookies of Restock's in-app browser. If the shop turns that away and you're not signed in to that shop in Restock, Restock may ask through its in-app browser instead, which then has no account of yours to send. If you are signed in to that shop in Restock, it never does: that shop is simply skipped for that scan. As with any website, the shop sees the barcode and your phone's internet address. A shop you've switched off is never asked.

OpenStreetMap (Nominatim at nominatim.openstreetmap.org, and the Overpass map servers overpass-api.de and overpass.private.coffee): when you look for nearby stores, the suburb or postcode you type, or your approximate position rounded to about 100 metres, is sent to find the closest supermarkets, bottle shops or pharmacies. The Overpass servers are asked one at a time: the second only if the first doesn't answer. Nothing else is sent.

The official recall lists (Food Standards Australia New Zealand, Product Safety Australia and the Therapeutic Goods Administration), while recall alerts are on: once a day, and when you tap Check now, Restock downloads their public lists of recent recalls, the same for everyone. Matching them against your items, list and receipts happens on your phone; nothing about you is sent.

No service, for receipts: the receipt reader (Tesseract, open-source text recognition, and its English model) is built into Restock since version 0.7.4, so nothing is downloaded. Receipts are read on your phone and never sent anywhere.

Store catalogue links open in your phone's web browser, under that website's own policy.

hotprices.org, only if you choose shared price history during setup or turn it on in Settings. It's an open-source project that records Coles and Woolworths prices every day. Restock downloads its public price files, the same for everyone, and keeps a short summary per product. Nothing about you or what you track is sent. Restock is testing this and plans to ask the project for permission before any public release.

GitHub (github.com), only when you tap Check for updates in Settings, or once a week if you turn on "Tell me when there's an update". Restock asks for the latest release's version number and notes, and if you tap Update, downloads the new APK from there. Nothing about you or what you track is sent.

## Backups

"Export a backup" makes one file with the parts you pick: each tab's items (with or without price history), shopping lists, loyalty cards (including their numbers), receipts (with or without their photos) and settings. You choose where it goes through Android's share menu. Keep it somewhere private, and don't post it publicly.

Automatic backups are off unless you turn them on. When they're on, Restock saves the same kind of file into a folder you pick, as often as you choose, and removes its own older automatic backups so only the newest few are kept. If that folder is synced by a cloud app, that app's own policy applies to the copies it keeps.

You can lock a backup with a password. The whole file is then encrypted on your phone, and nobody can open it without the password, including us and you if you forget it. Restock doesn't keep the password of a backup you export. The password for automatic backups, if you set one, is kept on your phone only and never put in a backup.

Just before a new version of Restock first opens, it saves a backup (without receipt photos) inside the app, so an update that changed something it shouldn't have can be undone. The last two are kept. If you use automatic backups, a copy also goes into that folder, locked if they are.

"Export as spreadsheets" makes CSV files of your pantry, shopping list and price history, and hands them to Android's share menu. Where they go is up to you.

With "Share prices about products" on (the default), a backup that includes price history also has a "shared prices" part: products and their prices only, for the items in that backup, nothing about you (see Sharing prices).

## Sync between your phones

Local sync is off unless you turn it on (Settings → Sync and backups → Local sync). When it's on, the phones in your household send each other the parts you choose to share (groceries, Keg and Pharmacy: their items, shopping list, prices, categories and alert settings), and loyalty card numbers if you choose to share them, directly over your own Wi-Fi. Parts you don't share stay on your phone. On the Wi-Fi, nothing goes through the internet or any server. The makers of Restock never see any of it.

Everything sent between the phones is encrypted with a key made on the phone that started the household (the owner phone). The key is never shown or put in a code: a phone joins by scanning a one-time code that works once and only for 10 minutes, and is then given the key over the encrypted link. A device without the key can't read what's sent or join in. When the owner removes a phone, the household's key changes so that phone can't sync again.

Each phone keeps its own copy. Stopping sync on a phone keeps what it has; to remove the household's things from a phone, delete them or uninstall Restock there.

Sync away from home is off unless you set it up: through your Nextcloud, another WebDAV server, or a folder on your phone that an app like Syncthing keeps the same. Each phone then writes one file there with the household's list, pantry, categories and alert settings, its shared loyalty cards if you chose to share them, and its kept receipts if the household shares them, sealed with the household's key so only your phones can read it. Prices and photos aren't included. If you sign in to Nextcloud, Nextcloud gives Restock a password of its own, so your own password never reaches Restock, and Restock asks Nextcloud to delete it when it stops using it. A WebDAV password you type in stays on your phone only. Restock only connects to a server over https, unless the server is on your home network or on Tailscale. Whoever stores the folder can see that the files are there, their size and when they change, but not what's in them. When the owner removes a phone, the owner also leaves the new key in the folder, sealed separately for each remaining phone.

The owner phone has a recovery key: 32 letters and numbers for you to keep somewhere safe. If the owner phone is lost, another phone in the household can take over as the owner with it. The other phones keep a sealed copy of what that needs, which only the recovery key opens. The key itself is only shown on the owner phone, after its screen lock. Restock never sends it anywhere, and we can't recover it for you.

Receipts are only shared if the owner phone turns on "Share receipts with the household" (it's off unless you do). Their details then go to the other phones like the rest of the household's things, for the tabs each phone shares, and their photos only go directly between the phones over your Wi-Fi. Phones only look for each other on Wi-Fi or a network cable, never over mobile data.

The household's keys, the secrets a takeover needs and any sync server password are sealed on each phone with a key kept in Android's key store. Each phone keeps a short record of which changes the others have seen, so it can tidy away old deletes after 90 days once every phone has them.

A future version may also sync through a server you run yourself, for example a Docker container on your own NAS. If you do that, your data lives on your server, and you're responsible for keeping it updated, backed up and secure.

## Sharing prices

Restock's plan is a free, open price list anyone can search: what things cost at each shop, and when. It's meant to be decentralised: the phones and computers running Restock share prices with each other, instead of one central server collecting them. The prices will never be sold.

"Share prices about products" is on unless you turn it off (Settings → Shared prices). For now nothing is sent anywhere. Restock only adds a "shared prices" part to the backups you export or save, ready for when sharing starts. Before any version sends prices off your phone, it will tell you, and you'll be able to say no.

What's in it: for each product Restock has checked at a shop, the shop, its name, brand, size, barcode and the shop's product number, and each price Restock read from the shop with the normal ("was") price, any special or multi-buy, and the day. Only the tabs in that backup are included. The products are the ones you track, but nothing in it links them to you. It never includes who you are, your loyalty cards, your branch or location, your shopping list or what you ticked off, your receipts, or anything you type about yourself. Products and prices you typed in yourself aren't included, and nor are products from shops you added yourself or sample items. Turning it off leaves it out of your future backups.
